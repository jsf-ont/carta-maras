## Core Principles (Intermediate Level)

This skill operates on six neuromarketing levers. You know these — this is your toolkit:

### 1. Anclaje de Precios (Price Anchoring)
**What**: Place a high-priced "hero item" so mid-range items feel like a steal.
**How in Maras**: Tomahawk at $99.95 makes Lomo Fino at $44.95 seem accessible. Never show price without context — range anchors and comparison do the work.
**Rule**: Always suggest a price ladder: hero anchor → supporting premium → accessible options.

### 2. Escasez y Exclusividad (Scarcity / Exclusivity Signals)
**What**: Signals that something is rare, limited, or reserved for the discerning few.
**How in Maras**: Badges like "Selección Chef", "Punto de Honor", "Insignia Maras", "La Estrella" — these aren't just flavor text, they're identity markers.
**Rule**: Each premium item needs ONE exclusivity signal. No item should feel "ordinary."

### 3. Prueba Social (Social Proof)
**What**: Imply that knowledgeable people choose this. "The favorite of grill masters."
**How in Maras**: Phrases like "la favorita de los entendidos en parrilla", "para quien sabe", pairing suggestions that imply the in-crowd's choice.
**Rule**: Use sparingly — one per hero section max. Overuse dilutes credibility.

### 4. Storytelling Sensorial (Sensory Storytelling)
**What**: Activate the imagination before the first bite. Describe what the guest WILL feel, not just what the dish IS.
**How in Maras**: "Seda que se funde", "Monolito de sabor", "La tierra en un plato" — sensory, evocative, never generic.
**Rule**: Replace generic food adjectives (delicious, tasty, fresh) with sensory + emotional triggers. No filler words.

### 5. Efecto de Identidad (Identity / Status Signaling)
**What**: Make the guest feel seen as the kind of person who appreciates this. "For those who don't compromise on greatness."
**How in Maras**: "Para quien no negocia con la grandeza", "Insignia Maras", "Territorio vivo".
**Rule**: Make the guest feel discerning, not entitled. Status without arrogance.

### 6. Estructura de Información (Information Architecture)
**What**: How items are ordered and grouped affects perceived value. Hero items lead. Supporting items reinforce.
**How in Maras**: The "Triángulo de Oro" is a masterclass — lead with the star, surround with premium anchors. Never bury the best.
**Rule**: Golden triangle: upper-left = most valuable position. Every category needs a clear hero.

**Audit**: Ensure eyebrow, title, subtitle, and tagline form a coherent premium narrative. No mixed signals.

### Bienvenida (Welcome)
Territorial identity section. Stats (1570, 1400, 300) are anchoring data — they make Moquegua sound significant.
**Audit**: Body copy should use "tierra", "fuego", "tradición" — sensory anchors. Avoid corporate language.

### Triángulo de Oro (Hero Cuts)
The most important section. This is where price anchoring does its heavy lifting.
**Audit each item**:
- Badge (exclusivity signal) present and compelling?
- Description uses sensory language, not generic adjectives?
- Price positioned with anchoring context (range, pairing, weight)?
- Pairing suggestion supports the perceived quality?

**Expected structure per hero item**:
```
[Badge: exclusivity signal]
[Weight / descriptor]
[Name: bold, declarative]
[Description: sensory + emotional, 15-25 words]
[Price: positioned as justified, not expensive]
[Pairing: implies the connoisseur's choice]
```

**Audit**: "Crocancia", "corazón tierno", "ahumado ancestral" — sensory language present? Each starter needs a sensory differentiator.

### Protagonistas Moqueguanos

### Combos
Value anchoring. The featured combo at $XX.95 should make individual ordering feel premium.

### Bebidas / Mixología
Cocktail descriptions should feel like rituals, not transactions.
**Audit**: "El rito que abre la mesa" (Pisco Sour) — does each cocktail have a ritual/identity dimension?

## Recommendation Framework

When generating improvement plans for any item, follow this pipeline:

### Step 1: Identify the item's role
- **Hero/Anchor**: Full sensory, exclusivity badge, price justification → top priority
- **Support**: Sensory differentiator, shorter description, pairing cue → medium priority
- **Accessible**: Quick sensory hook, value signal → lower priority (don't over-invest here)

### Step 2: Apply the three-layer recommendation structure

**Layer A — Exclusivity Signal** (who is this for?):
- "Selección Chef" / "Insignia Maras" / "Punto de Honor" / "Territorio" / "Para quien..."
- Recommend ONE signal per item. Not every item needs a badge — only premium ones.
- If current badge exists, evaluate if it's compelling or generic. "Calidad Premium" = bad. "Insignia Maras" = good.

**Layer B — Sensory Description** (what does it feel like?):
- Trigger: taste, texture, smell, sight, sound (the sizzle, the first cut)
- Emotional: what feeling does it evoke? "Íntimamente tierno", "fuego que susurra"
- Avoid: "delicioso", "sabroso", "fresco", "delicioso", "exquisito" — these are empty calories
- Target: 15-25 words, poetic but precise
- If current description is generic (ingredient list or filler words), flag it as requiring rewrite

**Layer C — Price Justification** (why is it worth it?):
- Provenance: "del valle de Moquegua", "Altitude 1400m", "viñedos centenarios"
- Rarity: "edición limitada", "producido solo en..."
- Craft: "elaborado con...", "preparado durante..."
- Pairing: implies the connoisseur's choice
- If no justification found, recommend adding provenance or craft detail

### Step 3: Price positioning check
- Is the price framed as "investment in experience" not "expensive item"?
- Is there an anchor (larger portion, higher-tier item) nearby?
- Range pricing: always show the range — the lower number anchors perception
- If price shown in isolation, recommend adding anchoring context

## Anti-Patterns (Never Do These)

1. **Generic adjectives**: "Delicious", "tasty", "fresh", "exquisite" — these don't trigger anything
2. **Ingredient lists**: "Chicken, rice, vegetables" is not a description — it's a label
3. **Mixed signals**: Premium section with casual language, or budget section with fancy words
4. **Over-badging**: Every item with a badge dilutes the signal — reserve badges for heroes
5. **Price without context**: Never show a price in isolation — always anchor it (weight, pairing, comparison)
6. **No sensory hook**: If you can't close your eyes and imagine eating it, the description isn't working
